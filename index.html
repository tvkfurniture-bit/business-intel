A disciplined process

From a local problem to a tested business

Research reduces uncertainty; it cannot eliminate risk. Use explicit decision gates before making large commitments.

STEP 01

Discover the problem

Identify a defined customer group, the problem they experience, and existing ways they solve it.

  • Public records and credible sources
  • Local competitor and substitute scan
  • Customer problem hypotheses
STEP 02

Evaluate the model

Separate source-backed facts from estimates and unknowns before estimating the resources required.

  • Startup and working-capital ranges
  • Revenue and cost assumptions
  • Operational and regulatory risks
STEP 03

Test demand cheaply

Seek real customer behaviour—not just likes, survey enthusiasm, or verbal encouragement.

  • Customer interviews and quotations
  • Paid pilot or pre-order where appropriate
  • Clear pass/fail criteria
STEP 04

Build the launch plan

Turn the tested concept into weekly tasks with cost limits and explicit decision points.

  • Supplier and channel shortlist
  • Licensing checks
  • Weekly milestones and owner
STEP 05

Run a controlled pilot

Start at a scale where a failed experiment is affordable and measurable.

  • Track enquiries and conversions
  • Measure contribution per sale
  • Review before increasing spend
STEP 06

Decide with evidence

Continue, modify, or stop based on the results and the original criteria—not sunk costs.

  • Document results and uncertainty
  • Compare actual vs. planned economics
  • Set the next go/no-go gate
Trust through transparency

Know what is fact, estimate, or still unknown.

Each detailed opportunity should make its evidence status visible. A polished report is not a substitute for reliable sources or direct customer validation.

What a complete opportunity report should contain

  • City, neighbourhood, customer segment, and problem statement
  • Sources with working links and access/review dates
  • Competitors, substitutes, and evidence of local supply
  • Itemized setup costs and working-capital assumptions
  • Revenue model, break-even assumptions, and key sensitivities
  • Licensing and safety checks to verify with relevant authorities
  • Low-cost demand test, pass/fail criteria, and next steps
  • Risks, unresolved questions, and reasons not to proceed

Never describe desk research as ground-verified unless actual field verification was completed and documented.

Frequently asked

Questions before you invest

Do you guarantee profit or business success?

No. Business outcomes depend on execution, customer behaviour, competition, costs, regulation, and many other factors. Reports support evaluation; they do not guarantee results.

Are all opportunities field-verified?

No. The evidence status should be shown on each opportunity. Desk research, third-party sources, estimates, and direct field verification are different evidence types and must not be conflated.

How should I interpret startup budgets?

As planning estimates, not quotations. Confirm local supplier prices, deposits, licences, taxes, staffing, logistics, and working capital before committing money.

What happens after I buy a report?

The actual delivery method, access duration, refund conditions, and support included must be clearly stated on the product and checkout pages before payment.
